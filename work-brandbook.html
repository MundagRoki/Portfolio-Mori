SmileLine · dental clinic

A brand book for a dental clinic, from the mark to the front door

SmileLine is a dental clinic. The mark is one open arc — a smile, drawn in a single stroke, with a spark where a tooth catches the light. The book takes that one shape and proves it survives everywhere it has to live: at the size of a business card, on an order form nobody looks twice at, and three metres wide on the front of the building.

The mark

primary and secondarygridclear space

Two lockups — stacked for signs and small spaces, horizontal for letterheads and anywhere the line is long. The arc is built on a circle, so it can be redrawn at any size without tracing, and the clear space is measured in the mark’s own units rather than millimetres, so it holds on a card and on a wall.

On paper

4 piecescard to order form

The pieces a patient actually ends up holding. The card carries the arc as a crop rather than a logo; the letter, the order form and the envelope each keep the mark small and let the arc do the work as a watermark and a fold line.

In place

shopfrontinterior

The test the rest of the book is written for. On the street the mark sits in a row of louder neighbours and still reads; inside, the arc is cut into the glass and lit, so the identity becomes the architecture rather than a sign hung on it.
